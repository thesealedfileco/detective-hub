The following statement was provided voluntarily by Ms. Evelyn Pike on October 16, 1998 at Moth Hollow Sheriff's Department. Statement recorded and transcribed. Ms. Pike was not under arrest at the time of this interview. She was advised of her right to have counsel present and declined.

Statement — Question and Answer Record

Q.1 Can you describe the evening of October 13th through the morning of October 14th at the station?
It was a regular shift, mostly. Naomi came in at her usual time — around eight in the evening. I was wrapping up from the afternoon. We talked briefly. She seemed tired, maybe a little on edge. I didn't think too much of it. She'd been a bit stressed the past few weeks. The phones had been ringing a lot, more than usual. I assumed listener calls — she had regular listeners that called in.
Q.2 What time did you leave the station that evening?
I stayed later than I planned. I was running checks on the transmitter log — routine maintenance paperwork. I think I left around 9:45around 10, maybe just before. I remember it was dark and the parking lot lights had come on.
[Interviewer note: witness corrected initial time estimate during statement]
Q.3 Was Naomi Vale still at the station when you left?
No. That's the thing. She left before I did. Which is odd, because her shift runs until one in the morning, normally. I just assumed she had the overnight programmed to tape, or she'd called someone in. It wasn't my concern — I'm not her supervisor. I locked up the engineering side and left.
Q.4 Did you speak with Naomi before she left?
Briefly. She said she had to go. Those were about her exact words — "I have to go." She had her bag already. She looked ... I don't know. She looked like she'd made up her mind about something. I didn't ask. I wish I had.
Q.5 Did you notice anything unusual at the station that evening — visitors, vehicles, anything out of the ordinary?
The phones, like I said. They kept ringing even after Naomi was already gone, which was strange, because the overnight line usually goes quiet if no one's on air. And one of the archive room lights was on when I went to check the transmitter log. I turned it off. I don't know who left it on. Could have been anyone.
Q.6 Is there anything else you think we should know about Naomi — her state of mind, any concerns she'd expressed recently?
She mentioned once, maybe two weeks before, that she'd found something in the archive room she didn't recognize. Tapes that weren't labeled in the station's format. I told her it was probably old stock from before my time. She didn't bring it up again. I should have asked more.
I, Evelyn Ruth Pike, confirm that the above statement is true and accurate to the best of my knowledge and recollection. I provide this statement voluntarily and without coercion.
Evelyn Pike
