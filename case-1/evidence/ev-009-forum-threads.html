Genuine question for anyone who has history with the area. Moth Hollow is a census-designated place that has been losing population steadily since the mid-1990s. It's not the only small WV community in that situation, but the rate of decline is steeper than comparable areas. The main employer in the region was always just local agriculture and a few service businesses. Nothing dramatic happened — no mine closure, no major flood.

Except the radio station going dark in 1998 and a missing person case that was never resolved.

I'm a WV history amateur and I find the area interesting. Anyone with local knowledge willing to share what it was like before and after?

I'm one of maybe 200 people still here so I can speak to this a bit. The town was never big, but there was more going on before. The radio station was actually a point of pride — WHLW had a decent reach and the overnight show in particular had listeners from well outside the county. People knew us from that.

After the host went missing and the station closed, it felt like something just went out. Hard to describe. People started leaving pretty quickly after that. Not because of the disappearance exactly. More like the thing that made it feel like a community was gone.

The relay tower is still there. Nobody's touched it. I don't know who owns it now.

No conclusion. Officially still open. The sheriff at the time — Mercer — ran the investigation. A lot of people thought it was closed too fast. Her family definitely did. There was a sister who was very vocal about it for a few years.

My understanding is that there were witness statement problems. Somebody's timeline didn't add up and nobody could agree on when she actually left the building. The case just kind of collapsed under the contradictions.

The station itself closed a few months after. Management said it was financial but everyone assumed it was connected.
