Preliminary notes

The following session records document participant responses over the course of the ████████████████████ study. All participants provided consent under the Community Outreach Program agreement. Identifying information has been removed from this copy per institutional protocol.

Broadcast material was prepared in coordination with ████████ and delivered via standard overnight transmission during scheduled programming hours. Participants were not informed of the conditioning component of the study design.

Session records — subject group A
Session 04  ·  October 1994

Subjects in Group A report unprompted awareness of ████ during standard listening sessions. Three of seven subjects described a low-frequency sound — referred to independently as "a hum" or "humming." No audio stimulus corresponding to this description was present in the broadcast material for this session.

This is consistent with the expected onset of associative suggestion response. The absence of an external stimulus producing the reported experience is not unexpected at this stage of exposure.

Session 07  ·  March 1995

Increased suggestibility confirmed across all Group A subjects following three consecutive exposure sessions. Language adoption is progressing at a rate ████████████ above baseline projections.

Subjects have begun using introduced language without prompting. The phrase "you hear it too" was recorded in unsolicited written communication between two Group A subjects who had not previously made direct contact. Neither subject appeared aware they were using introduced phrasing. Phrase conditioning is functioning as designed — subjects begin self-reinforcing with introduced language. The group becomes its own transmission mechanism.

Session 11  ·  November 1995

Anomalous result requiring documentation. Subject ███, identified as a non-exposed control participant, reported experiencing ██████████████████████ consistent with Group A symptom profile. Subject used the phrase "the humming" without prior exposure to Group A broadcast materials.

Spontaneous adoption by non-exposed control subjects was not projected at this stage of the study. This may indicate ████████████████████████████████████████. Finding has been flagged for separate documentation and will not appear in the primary study summary.

Session records — subject group B / broadcast integration phase
Session 14  ·  February 1996

Group B subjects introduced to ████████████████ via the overnight broadcast window. The call-in format provides a naturalistic interaction context that reduces subject resistance significantly — subjects do not conceptualize passive listening as participation in a structured study.

Memory shaping is progressing as expected. Subjects in the second week of exposure are beginning to report experiences consistent with suggestion rather than direct experience. Two subjects described visiting ████████████ and encountering phenomena they attribute to the broadcast. No such visit is corroborated by ████████████████████.

Session 19  ·  January 1997

The overnight host has become a significant variable in Group B outcomes. ████████████████████████████████████. Her relationship with regular callers is more personal than the study design anticipated. Subjects in Group B reference her directly by name in unsolicited communications. Several repeat phrases she has used on-air verbatim — phrases not introduced as conditioning material by the study.

Emotional vulnerability increases susceptibility significantly. Subjects who reported personal distress during the study period showed ████████████ higher language adoption rates than the general Group B population. The host's own apparent anxiety — communicated through vocal pacing, unscripted pauses, audible tension during caller interactions — may be functioning as an unintended conditioning signal for the listener group.

This variable was not designed into the study. Its effects will be monitored but not corrected at this stage.

Session 22  ·  September 1997

Reference to "the tower" has entered subject language without introduction from study materials. Origin unclear. Three Group B subjects and one control subject have independently used this phrase in the context of ██████████████████████████. The phrase does not appear in any prepared broadcast material.

Possible sources: ████████████████████████████████████████████████████████. Organic propagation of location-specific association is a significant finding. No corrective material will be introduced at this stage.

Session 24  ·  April 1998

Study integrity is under pressure. The host has begun asking questions that suggest awareness of ████████████████████████. She has not made direct contact but her on-air behavior has shifted — questions to callers are more pointed, her responses more guarded. She appears to be ████████████████████████████████.

Closing observations — October 1998

The following notes were appended to the study file in October 1998 and represent the final entries before the study was suspended.

Final entry  ·  October 1998

Study materials have been secured. The primary record — ████████████████████████████████████████████ — has been removed from the station archive and is no longer accessible through standard channels.

Subject responses across the full study period demonstrate that tonal repetition combined with controlled emotional context produces measurable, durable changes in recall, perception, and language behavior across a general listening population. The broadcast medium is effective as a delivery mechanism because subjects do not conceptualize passive listening as participation. Results exceed projections across all primary metrics.

The questions raised by the host's ████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████████ have been resolved. No further documentation is required on that matter.

The study is complete. Results will be archived separately under ████████████████. This copy should not exist outside this office.
